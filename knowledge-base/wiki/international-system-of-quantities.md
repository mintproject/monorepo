# International System of Quantities (ISQ)

**Summary**: ISO 80000, the physics-based companion to SI units, defining 7 base quantities from which a vast system of derived quantities and their dimensions is built.

**Sources**: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-06

---

The **International System of Quantities (ISQ)**, or **ISO 80000**, is the companion standard to the International System of Units (SI). It provides a general, unifying, physics-rooted system for assigning numerical values and units to a very broad class of [[property-names|quantitative properties]] (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

## Seven base quantities

None can be expressed in terms of the others; together they form the basis of a vast system of derived quantities (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf):

| Base quantity | Symbol | Dimension | SI unit | Unit symbol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| length | l | L | metre | m |
| mass | m | M | kilogram | kg |
| time | t | T | second | s |
| electric current | I | I | ampere | A |
| thermodynamic temperature | T | Θ | kelvin | K |
| amount of substance | n | N | mole | mol |
| luminous intensity | Iv | J | candela | cd |

The paper notes "currency" is sometimes added as an eighth base quantity, accommodating units like dollars per gallon (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

## Dimensions of derived quantities

The dimension ("generic units") of any derived quantity is a product of base-dimension symbols raised to real-number exponents (negative, positive, or zero; zero exponents omitted). Examples (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf):

- velocity [L T⁻¹]
- force [L M T⁻²] (Newton)
- pressure [L⁻¹ M T⁻²] (Pascal)
- energy [L² M T⁻²] (Joule)
- power [L² M T⁻³] (Watt)
- voltage [L² M T⁻¹ I⁻¹] (Volt)
- frequency [T⁻¹] (Hertz)

**Dimensionless quantities** have dimension symbol 1 — often a ratio of same-dimension quantities (slope = rise/run [L/L]), but also angles in radians and integer counts. The ISQ also recognizes dimensionless **levels** on logarithmic scales, such as sound pressure level (decibel) and information entropy (nat) (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).
