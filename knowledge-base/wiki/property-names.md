# Property Names

**Summary**: Properties of objects/phenomena split into quantitative (quantities) and qualitative (measured via indexes); both follow a linguistic rule of nominalizing adjectives into nouns.

**Sources**: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-06

---

The properties of objects and phenomena divide into two categories: **quantitative properties** (also called quantities) and **qualitative properties**. Both are observable, but quantitative properties can be quantified or compared directly with numbers and often have measurement units rooted in physics, whereas qualitative properties are not directly measurable with a physics-based unit system (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

## Quantitative properties

Grounded in the [[international-system-of-quantities]] (ISQ / ISO 80000), a physics-based system that assigns numerical values and units to a broad class of quantitative properties (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

## Qualitative properties, indexes, and indicators

Many properties (e.g., a person's intelligence, attractiveness, obesity, creditworthiness) cannot be directly quantified with a physics-based system. When a specific method is introduced to compute and assign a numerical value to such a property, it is called an **index**, scale, or score (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

Indexes are typically computed from several other properties called **indicators** — indicative of the qualitative property but insufficient alone. Examples (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf):

- **BMI** (obesity) — from 2 indicators: height and weight; body mass [kg] / height² [kg/m²].
- **FICO Score** (creditworthiness) — 5 indicators: payment history, debt burden, length of credit history, types of credit, recent credit searches (weights 35/30/15/10/10%).
- **DJIA** (economic health) — stock prices of 30 prominent companies.
- **NDVI** (vegetation, remote sensing).
- **IQ / SAT** (intelligence).

Indexes act as **pseudo-quantities** for monitoring and comparison, applied to objects and phenomena from people to markets to countries (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

## Property Name Rule

The vast majority of English property names are generated by **nominalizing an adjective** (converting a descriptive word to a noun), often via "ness" or "ity" (attractiveness, generosity). An old-English set ends in "th": wide→width, deep→depth, long→length, strong→strength, high→height, broad→breadth (source: Peckham_and_Stoica_2022_ICWRER_2022.pdf).

This parallels the [[process-names]] Process Name Rule (nominalizing verbs).
